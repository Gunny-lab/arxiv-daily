### Spin entanglement in $pp$ and $pA$ collisions: high-energy TMD effects and fragmentation probes

- Matched keywords: title: entanglement, spin; abstract: amplitudes, density matrix, entanglement, helicity, helicity amplitudes, quantum, spin, spin correlations

**Abstract**

Spin-spin correlations of a quark-antiquark pair produced in proton-proton or proton-Ion collisions are investigated in $k_T$-factorization within the shockwave formalism. We construct the full spin-density matrix of both inclusive and diffractive channels in the limit in which the two quarks are back-to-back. In the inclusive channel, due to linearly polarized gluons, we show that a $\cos (2 φ)$-modulation of the concurrence, stabilizer Rényi entropy, and quantum discord emerges and depends on the interplay between six small-$x$ gluon TMDs. The modulation is predicted to be maximally strong and independent of the transverse momentum imbalance, $q$, from a dilute (BFKL) perspective, while it is considerably suppressed by saturation effects. In diffraction, we observe that the pair is always entangled and exhibits the same properties found in diffractive DIS. We explain how these properties follow directly from the exclusive and eikonal nature of the Pomeron exchange. We trace this behavior to a spin-density matrix of rank at most two with maximally mixed marginals. For this class of states, entanglement and Bell nonlocality coincide even when the state is mixed, while the explicit form of the light-front helicity amplitudes ensures that both persist in the whole phase space. We suggest accessing the spin correlations in unpolarized hadron collisions through the Collins and dihadron fragmentation functions. We point out that, in inclusive production, for massless quarks, suitably chosen double-Collins asymmetries provide promising entanglement witnesses.

**Abstract**

We present a training-free method that simplifies massless spinor-helicity expressions by a greedy best-first search over on-shell identities, and test it at four, five, and six points. Applied to the color-ordered five-gluon MHV tree-level amplitude, the search derives the Parke-Taylor formula from all 1024 Feynman-diagram expressions with different polarization reference spinors and up to 228 numerator terms. On public test sets used by previous machine-learning methods, the search solves every case and generally outperforms these methods in solve rate. Its mean wall-clock time per case is 0.02-0.3 s on one CPU core. With longer searches and an alternative ranking of candidate expressions, it also solves all 21,600 cases of a new suite of larger test expressions. Each result comes with an explicit derivation and is independently checked over finite fields at on-shell kinematics. For the classes studied here, the method effectively compresses long expressions produced by identity scrambling or Feynman-diagram calculations into compact forms. This is a first step toward extracting physics from amplitudes by relating distinct compact expressions, selecting representations with preferred factors and poles, and guiding the discovery of new amplitude structures.

**Abstract**

We study decoherence of a scalar field coupled to ``anomalous'' environments with critical behavior with the Schwinger-Keldysh including the influence functional, and the quantum master equation (QME) up to second order in the coupling. The Schwinger-Keldysh formulation yields a Langevin equation and the Lindblad (QME) includes \emph{time dependent rates} allowing to study transient dynamics. We focus on three examples featuring non-Markov dynamics that cannot be described with S-matrix/Fermi's golden rule rates \textbf{i) Unparticle physics:} a conformal sector beyond the Standard Model at an infrared fixed point featuring correlation functions with anomalous scaling dimensions, \textbf{ii) Threshold and infrared enhancement:} when the mass shell of the field coincides with or is near to a kinematic threshold of the spectral density of the bath, \textbf{iii) Critical environments:} the bath is at a critical point, correlation functions scale with anomalous dimensions and long-wavelength fluctuations feature critical slowing down. In all cases the reduced density matrix undergoes decoherence in the energy basis of the probe, and off-diagonal matrix elements decay as $e^{-Φ(t)}$ with $Φ(t)>0 $ the \emph{decoherence function} and $Γ(t) = \dotΦ(t)$ the decoherence rate. In all cases we find that either transient or asymptotically $Φ(t) \propto t^{-μ}~;~ μ>0$ with $μ$, determined by the behavior of the spectral density at threshold and the anomalous scaling dimensions of the correlation functions, or $Φ(t) \propto \ln(t)$ for the infrared case. For a critical environment there is a new dynamical scaling exponent. A probe of frequency $ω_q$ is resonant with a \emph{critically slow} quasiparticle for $q_*\propto ω^{1/z}_q$ at which decoherence is strongly \emph{enhanced}.

**Abstract**

A non-supersymmetric $SU(5)$ grand unified model is constructed based on the non-holomorphic modular $S'_4$ flavor symmetry, supplemented by a global Peccei--Quinn $U(1)_{PQ}$ symmetry that constrains the Yukawa couplings. Constructed from polyharmonic Maaß forms, these couplings allow the model to reproduce the observed charged-fermion masses and mixing as well as the neutrino oscillation parameters for both normal ordering (NO) and inverted ordering (IO), with all Pull values satisfying $|\mathcal{P}| < 0.8$. The model further yields specific predictions for yet-unmeasured parameters, including the absolute neutrino masses, the Dirac CP phase, the Majorana phases, and the effective Majorana mass $m_{ββ}$. We then study thermal leptogenesis by solving the density matrix equations. For a given benchmark point, the final baryon asymmetry depends solely on the single free parameter $b$ that sets the right-handed neutrino mass scale, and an appropriate choice of this parameter reproduces the observed value $η_B \simeq 6.12 \times 10^{-10}$. Finally, the correlation between this scale parameter and specific low-energy observables provides a potential bridge between the fermion flavor structure and early-Universe cosmology.

**Abstract**

The spin correlation of $Λ\barΛ$ hyperon pairs produced in heavy-ion collisions is investigated through scalar and pseudo-scalar mesonic channels that mediate the creation of $s\bar{s}$ pairs. Because a $J=0$ meson forces $L=S$ while parity requires $(-1)^{L+1}=+/-1$, the two vertices project the pair onto distinct total-spin states: a scalar ($0^+$) vertex selects the $L=S=1$ configuration with a spin weight of $+1$, whereas a pseudo-scalar ($0^-$) vertex selects the $L=S=0$ configuration with a weight of $-3$; these weights are the traces of the correlation tensor, corresponding to $P_{s\bar{s}}=+1/3$ and $-1$, respectively. Furthermore, we show that the collision-energy dependence of the momentum-integrated spin correlation along the freeze-out line is sensitive to the trajectory's path relative to the chiral phase boundary. Trajectories passing near the crossover region exhibit a non-monotonic structure, providing a sensitive probe for the location of the chiral phase transition.

**Abstract**

Establishing jet quenching in small collision systems, such as high-multiplicity proton-proton collisions at the LHC, is challenging because the expected modifications are small and can be obscured by event-selection effects, multiparton interactions, and multijet production. These findings quantify the sensitivity of the studied observables to medium-induced jet modifications in the present pp calculations.

**Abstract**

The reconstruction of the top-quark-pair ($t\bar{t}$) kinematics in the dilepton $t\bar{t}$ decay channel is challenging due to the presence of two undetected neutrinos. In this work, existing kinematic reconstruction methods used by the CMS Collaboration are studied using a Monte Carlo sample with $t\bar{t}$ events from CMS Open Data at a centre-of-mass energy of 7 TeV.
