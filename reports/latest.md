**Abstract**

We investigate timelike Compton scattering (TCS) on a deuteron target as a probe of the three-dimensional partonic structure of the deuteron encoded in generalized parton distributions (GPDs). Such measurements may provide new insight into the internal structure of the deuteron, and help to clarify the unresolved tensor-polarization puzzle. We present numerical estimates of the leading-order (in the strong coupling $α_s$) TCS cross section in the kinematic region relevant to future measurements at Jefferson Lab. The deuteron GPDs are evaluated within a convolution model, while the Bethe-Heitler contribution is calculated using a phenomenological parametrization of the deuteron electromagnetic form factors. In addition, we derive the theoretical expressions for the next-to-leading-order corrections to deuteron TCS, with particular emphasis on contributions from the gluon transversity GPDs. We show that these contributions can be isolated through their characteristic angular dependence in the center-of-mass frame of the produced lepton pair. Our results demonstrate the potential of deuteron TCS as a complementary probe of both the quark and gluon structure of the deuteron at Jefferson Lab.

**Abstract**

We study light top-flavoured dark matter (DM) below the electroweak (EW) scale within the dark Standard Model effective field theory (DSMEFT). We perform the one-loop matching of the DSMEFT onto the dark low-energy effective field theory (DLEFT) at the EW scale for all relevant operators that couple scalar, fermionic or vector DM, as well as axion-like particles, exclusively to third-generation quarks. After QCD evolution to the relevant low-energy scales, we match the DLEFT onto the non-relativistic EFT for DM-nucleus scattering. This EFT chain provides a unified framework for phenomenological studies of rare $B$- and $K$-meson decays with missing energy, direct and indirect detection, and the thermal relic abundance. Interpreting the Belle II excess in $B^+\to K^+ν\barν$ as a decay into DM, we find that only the DSMEFT operators contributing at tree level can accommodate it with $O(1)$ couplings for a new-physics scale of $1\,\mathrm{TeV}$. The decay $B^0\to K^{*0}+\mathrm{inv}$ provides the strongest constraint over large portions of the Belle II-favoured parameter space, while $K^+\toπ^++\mathrm{inv}$ can dominate at low and $B_s\to\mathrm{inv}$, $B^+\toπ^++\mathrm{inv}$ and $Υ(1S)\to\mathrm{inv}$ become important at high DM masses, respectively. Direct-detection searches by CRESST-III and DarkSide-50 are competitive for operators involving scalar quark currents and, depending on the operator, exclude the Belle~II-favoured band for $m_{\mathrm{DM}}\gtrsim1$-$2\,\mathrm{GeV}$. We also perform detailed analyses of the thermal relic abundance and the indirect-detection constraints from the CMB and Fermi-LAT.

**Abstract**

We present a complete dispersive evaluation of the Bernabéu-Ericson-Ferro Fontan-Tarrach (BEFT) sum rule for the nucleon dipole polarizability difference $α_{E1}-β_{M1}$. The analysis is based on hyperbolic dispersion relations, which separate the $s$- and $t$-channel contributions without double counting and are particularly suited to backward real Compton scattering. This framework makes it possible to precisely determine the polarizability difference from independent dispersive input rather than from fits to low-energy Compton scattering data. The $s$-channel part is updated with modern single-pion photoproduction multipoles and model estimates of non-$πN$ inelastic channels. For the $t$-channel, we use contemporary dispersive input for $γγ\toππ/K\bar K_{I_t=0}$ together with Roy-Steiner equation solutions for $ππ/K\bar K_{I_t=0}\to N\bar N$, and we extend the calculation to the isovector $πη/K\bar K_{I_t=1}$ channel associated with the $a_0(980)$. The resulting treatment resolves the long-standing puzzle of earlier BEFT evaluations driven by the scalar-isoscalar two-pion channel and gives a controlled estimate of the subleading isovector contribution. Combining the resulting BEFT values with the Baldin sum rule, we obtain, in units of $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{fm}^3$, \[ α_{E1}^p=11.17(52),\quad β_{M1}^p=2.84(52),\quad α_{E1}^n=12.18(59),\quad β_{M1}^n=3.03(59). \] For the proton, these values provide a benchmark with a precision comparable to state-of-the-art dispersive and chiral effective field theory extractions. For the neutron, whose polarizabilities are much less directly constrained experimentally, the result provides an independent dispersive benchmark with an uncertainty comparable to that of the proton.

**Abstract**

We present an updated global analysis of the transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) helicity distributions of the nucleon from measurements of the longitudinal double-spin asymmetry $A_{LL}$ in semi-inclusive deep-inelastic scattering (SIDIS). The analysis incorporates target- and detected-hadron-mass corrections, next-to-next-to-leading-order matching in the small-$b_T$ operator product expansion, and an improved treatment of correlated systematic uncertainties. The present data constrain the up- and down-quark helicity TMDs, whereas the sea-quark and gluon distributions remain weakly constrained. We also provide predictions for SIDIS $A_{LL}$ at CLAS12 and for longitudinal single-spin asymmetries in $W^\pm$ production with leptonic decays at STAR kinematics. An impact study with pseudodata for the proposed Electron-Ion Collider in China (EicC) indicates substantial potential to improve constraints on helicity TMDs at intermediate $x$, including the presently weakly constrained sea-quark sector.

**Abstract**

Resonant scattering links particle interactions to the formation and decay of unstable excitations. We develop a hybrid quantum-computing framework to study these dynamics through fermion--antifermion collisions. Our formulation of $(1+1)$-dimensional Yukawa theory encodes fermions in qubits and the scalar field in qumodes. We identify the operations needed for a photonic implementation and benchmark the dynamics with tensor-network simulations. These results establish a concrete scattering benchmark for hybrid quantum computers and demonstrate how local real-time observables can probe particle-resonance dynamics.

**Abstract**

We investigate the fermion spectra and collider phenomenology of models based on the \(SU(3)_C \otimes SU(4)_L \otimes U(1)_X\) gauge symmetry, focusing on two realizations of the electric charge operator characterized by \(b=c=1\) and \(b=1,\ c=-2\). For each configuration, we classify anomaly-free fermion sets and identify five minimal spectra with at least three SM-like quark and lepton families for each configuration, distinguishing algebraically irreducible structures from reducible spectra that remain minimal under the three-family requirement. We derive the chiral couplings of the additional neutral gauge boson \(Z'\) to Standard Model fermions and show that distinct anomaly-free fermion spectra can lead to identical low-energy neutral-current interactions. We then investigate the corresponding collider constraints using the ATLAS dilepton resonance search at \(\sqrt{s}=13\) TeV with an integrated luminosity of \(139\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}\), assuming negligible \(Z-Z'\) mixing and kinematically inaccessible exotic fermions. A particularly interesting result emerges for the \(b=c=1\) configuration, where all \(Z'\) couplings to Standard Model fermions are proportional to \(\sinψ\), with \(ψ\) denoting the additional neutral gauge-boson mixing angle. Consequently, these couplings vanish simultaneously at \(ψ=nπ\), giving rise to a fermiophobic limit in which conventional Drell-Yan dilepton constraints disappear. In contrast, the \(b=1,\ c=-2\) configuration does not exhibit a universal fermiophobic limit. Our results demonstrate that collider constraints on additional neutral gauge bosons depend sensitively on the fermion embeddings and neutral gauge-boson mixing structure, and suggest the possibility of comparatively light \(Z'\) bosons in suitable realizations of extended electroweak gauge theories.

**Abstract**

We compute the entanglement entropy of a discretized scalar field in the fixed spacetime background of an evolving wormhole. Evolving wormholes are a class of time dependent and spherically symmetric wormhole geometries. In both expanding and contracting models, we find dynamically generated entanglement. Asymptotically far from the wormhole throat, the entanglement entropy is well described by the leading term in the area law, although its coefficient can depend sensitively on the value of the UV cutoff. As the UV cutoff is decreased, the coefficient becomes progressively less sensitive to the cutoff, providing numerical evidence that it approaches a cutoff-independent value in the continuum limit. Near the throat, we find a double-dip structure in the entanglement entropy which cannot be described by the leading and logarithmic contributions to the area law.

**Abstract**

The Summers-Werner theorem shows that the vacuum of a relativistic quantum field theory violates the Bell-CHSH inequality maximally on complementary wedges, but it is an existence statement: it exhibits neither the observables nor an apparatus that measures them. We give an explicit operator-level realization within the Fewster-Verch measurement framework, obtaining dichotomic Bell observables of a free Majorana field in $1+1$ dimensions as observables induced by local couplings to Majorana probes. **Abstract**

We present novel graph-centered algorithms for the construction and representation of worldline effective field theory (WEFT) integrands that separate the topological and algebraic structure of loop integrands into independent graph-numerator pairs. **Abstract**

This thesis investigates the perturbative dynamics and phenomenology of compact objects in two approaches to quantum gravity: quadratic gravity and asymptotic safety. Overall, this work demonstrates how the perturbative dynamics of compact objects can connect quantum gravity models to potentially observable astrophysical phenomena.

**Abstract**

We review neutrino mass as a probe of restored parity in left-right symmetric theories, emphasizing the distinct Majorana and Dirac realizations. In the Majorana theory, lepton-number violation connects the seesaw mechanism to neutrinoless double beta decay, heavy-neutrino production and the Keung-Senjanovic process. The Dirac alternative removes these characteristic signals while retaining a relation between left- and right-handed flavor. The right-handed leptonic mixing matrix can then be reconstructed from low-energy data and the spontaneous parity-breaking parameter. Requiring the two lepton Yukawa matrices to admit a common real parity basis turns this reconstruction into a compatibility condition that determines the allowed Dirac phase on each branch. For three generations, this requirement reduces to a single rephasing-invariant CP-odd obstruction, which also governs the right-left modulus mismatch and the relative CP orientation. The small Dirac masses also restrict the parity deformation and its induced strong-CP phase. For compressed mixed-sign neutrino spectra, a tiny parity deformation can nevertheless generate large leptonic CP violation, despite stringent strong-CP constraints. The resulting branch-dependent correlations connect leptonic CP, the absolute neutrino mass and strong CP. We place this construction alongside radiative and unified approaches to predicting leptonic CP violation.

### Higher-twist hadronic tensor in deep inelastic scattering with massive quarks

- arXiv: [2610.32387v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32387v1) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32387v1)

- Relevance score: 15 (medium)

**Abstract**

In this paper, we calculate the inclusive deep inelastic scattering (DIS) with finite quark masses up to twist-4 accuracy based on collinear expansion method. The collinear expansion method not only naturally leads to gauge-invariant correlation functions and parton distribution functions, but also yields a remarkably compact expression for the hadronic tensor. The resulting hadronic tensor can be organized according to the corresponding power behavior in $1/Q$. In particular, the explicit quark-mass dependence gives rise to additional power-suppressed contributions and the ratio $m/Q$ can be counted as a twist-3 contribution in the power expansion. As an application, we further investigate the role of transversity in inclusive DIS with a transversely polarized target. We find that the transversity distribution appears explicitly in the quark-quark correlation function and gives rise to a contribution proportional to $m~h_{1T}/Q$. However, this contribution is exactly cancelled in the hadronic tensor by the one arising from the corresponding quark-gluon-quark correlation function. This cancellation shows that, within the one-photon-exchange approximation, the mass-induced transversity term cancels in the complete twist-3 hadronic tensor, and no net transversity contribution remains at twist-3 accuracy in inclusive DIS. We complement this analytical study with numerical estimates of the $g_T$-related twist-3 asymmetry, comparing predictions with and without the mass-dependent $x\rightarrowχ$ rescaling at the same measured $x$. The results show that these kinematic mass effects become more important at lower $Q^2$ and larger $x$.

**Abstract**

Topological defects are a generic prediction of field theories with spontaneously broken symmetries, and domain walls, the two-dimensional defects left by a broken discrete symmetry, are among the simplest. We study the imprint of dark, purely gravitationally interacting domain walls on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), in the regime where a low symmetry-breaking scale $η$ allows the walls to evade the Zel'dovich overclosure bound while remaining cosmologically relevant. The relativistic repulsion of matter by a planar wall carves out an evacuated void bounded by an overdense shell, and we compute the resulting photon deflection field analytically for walls both aligned with and arbitrarily inclined to the line of sight. Casting these methods as sensitivity forecasts for a South Pole Telescope-like (SPT) survey, we find that the curvelet and wavelet statistical thresholds populate a common $η\sim 10-100$ MeV band over the accessible redshift range. The neural-ratio sensitivity curve, which exploits the full map morphology, reaches a factor 1.3-1.9 lower in $η$ at $z \gtrsim 20$, or $\sim 2-7$ in the wall tension, landing essentially on the per-resolution-element noise floor.

**Abstract**

In electrodynamics, and more generally in gauge theories, quantisation introduces an ambiguity whereby the vector current, the axial current, or both may acquire an anomalous divergence. These anomalies can be classified as relevant, conventionally associated with the infrared properties of the theory, and irrelevant, arising from the freedom in the choice of renormalisation scheme. We show that, under a axial reparametrisation of the fermion fields, the global axial symmetry can be promoted to a gauge redundancy. Requiring this gauge symmetry to be preserved at the quantum level eliminates the irrelevant anomalies, leaving a non-propagating pseudoscalar field that acts as a Lagrange multiplier enforcing the familiar axial anomaly. We further argue that, on topologically non-trivial spacetimes, in particular those with non-trivial second cohomology, this axial gauge symmetry can undergo a Higgs mechanism, rendering the pseudoscalar field an emergent propagating degree of freedom. Finally, we briefly point out an analogous emergent state associated with the conformal anomaly.
