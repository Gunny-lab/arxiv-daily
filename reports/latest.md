**Abstract**

A QCD sum-rule study is performed to place bounds on the leading-order (LO) hadronic vacuum polarization (HVP) contribution to the anomalous magnetic moment of the $τ$ lepton ($a_τ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO},\, a_τ= (g-2)_τ/2$). The QCD contributions to $a_τ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO}$ are modeled as a combination of Laplace QCD sum rules (LSRs) and finite-energy QCD sum rules (FESRs) of various weights, which are bounded by Hölder and related fundamental inequalities. The light-quark ($u,\,d,\,s$) contributions are evaluated up to five-loop order in perturbative contributions in the chiral limit, LO in light quark mass corrections, next-to-leading order (NLO) in dimension-four QCD condensates and to LO in dimension-six QCD condensates. Extending previous work on $a_μ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO}$, we present a robust methodology for using QCD sum rules to constrain $a_τ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO}$, and more generally any lepton $g-2$. A criterion is developed to extract from the bounds analysis a final result of $a_τ^\mathrm{QCD} = \left( 3.385\pm 0.048 \right) \times 10^{-6}$, which after inclusion of charmonium and bottomonium contributions gives a final result $a_τ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO} = \left( 3.501\pm0.048\right) \times 10^{-6}$, comparable to data-driven and lattice predictions. Lattice QCD results along with our value for $a_τ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO}$ are used to obtain a combined QCD determination from lattice and sum-rule methods of $a_μ^\mathrm{HVP,\,LO} = (7.217\pm0.233)\times10^{-8}$. Our analysis provides fundamental QCD sum-rule inputs into the theoretical determinations of $a_τ$, and establishes new methodologies for QCD sum-rule determinations of low-energy observables.

**Abstract**

Effective field theory is a powerful tool for studying the low-energy behavior of dark sector models without explicit dependence on the high-energy physics. However, bottom-up EFT analyses can miss important processes in UV completions that are relevant for dark matter production and detection. In this work we demonstrate explicitly that for Majorana dark matter coupled to the photon by the dimension-six anapole operator, different UV completions make qualitatively different predictions at high energy colliders, in the early universe, and in direct detection experiments. Additionally, we show that competitive, and in some cases, leading constraints on anapole dark matter can be derived by searching for signatures of additional particles in the UV completion rather than direct probes of the DM itself. We argue that the UV completions studied in this work have categorically different phenomenologies and that `model-independent' EFT constraints are not always applicable.

**Abstract**

We present a lattice-QCD determination of nucleon-nucleon scattering amplitudes at pion masses of $M_π\approx 200$ MeV and $M_π\approx 280$ MeV at a single lattice spacing $a \approx 0.064$ fm on two CLS ensembles. The finite-volume energy spectra are extracted from large Hermitian correlation matrices constructed within the distillation framework using an extensive basis of two-nucleon interpolating operators. To reliably relate the finite-volume spectra to the infinite-volume scattering amplitudes, we employ two recently developed formalisms that explicitly account for the long-range interaction generated by one-pion exchange: the plane-wave quantization condition and the relativistic left-hand-cut formalism. **Abstract**

We investigate non-invertible completions of anomalous flavor symmetries and their application to Dirac neutrino masses. We formulate symmetry defects dressed by topological quantum field theories for flavor symmetries including finite non-Abelian flavor transformations with rational anomaly characters and modular flavor symmetries with the rational part of modular anomaly cocycles, for gauge sectors with the required exact higher-form symmetry. As a concrete example, we construct a Froggatt-Nielsen (FN) model in which an order-four subgroup $\mathbb Z_4^{\rm FN}\subset U(1)_{\rm FN}$ is completed into a non-invertible defect through its mixed anomaly with an intermediate hidden $U(1)_H$ gauge sector in the infrared monopole-decoupling limit. The non-invertible FN symmetry can be explicitly broken at the energy scale where monopoles become dynamical. This breaking from the infrared viewpoint can be realized in an ultraviolet hidden $SU(2)_H$ theory as instanton effects, where a constrained instanton with topological charge one generates a fermion mixing term that violates FN charge by one unit. Its coefficient contains the nonperturbative factor $ζ_H\sim\exp[-8π^2/g_H^2(v_H)]$. When independent FN-breaking terms are subleading, integrating out the heavy fermions gives $(Y_ν)_{ij}\proptoζ_Hε^{\ell_i+n_j}$ in a rotated basis for the right-handed neutrinos, to leading order in the instanton-induced fermion mixing. The nonperturbative factor provides the exponential suppression of the neutrino Yukawa couplings, while the FN charges determine the powers of $ε$. For the chosen FN charges and generic order-one coefficients, $m_2/m_3\simε$, favoring normal ordering. An independent $\mathbb Z_8^D$ gauge symmetry forbids Majorana masses. The same FN charge assignment reproduces the leading quark and charged-lepton Yukawa hierarchies.

**Abstract**

We present a stochastic calculus approach for modeling generalized parton distribution functions using information provided from lattice QCD calculations of Mellin moments, without assuming a functional form. Reconstructing the underlying dependence on the partonic momentum fraction from a limited number of moments constitutes an intrinsically non-unique inverse problem. Our goal is to provide a model agnostic way to determine GPDs subject to integral constraints. **Abstract**

We investigate the timelike entanglement entropy (TEE) in a two-dimensional conformal field theory with a gravitational anomaly, $c_L\neq c_R$, dual to topologically massive gravity in AdS$_3$. Holographically, the TEE is determined by the on-shell action of a massive spinning particle on a single complex geodesic anchored at the endpoints of the timelike interval. This action consists of the complex geodesic length and the twist of a parallel-transported normal frame. We carry out the computation in Poincaré AdS$_3$, non-extremal and extremal rotating BTZ backgrounds, and provide an equivalent Chern-Simons derivation in which the bulk connection stays real while only the saddle of the Wilson-line probe becomes complex. In each case, the holographic result matches the corresponding TEE in dual field theory. The real part of the TEE is fixed unambiguously whereas its imaginary part depends on the boundary $iε$ prescription and the orientation of the interval. In the bulk, the dependence on orientation reflects an ambiguity in the choice of framing. Along the complex geodesic, the residual frame group becomes $SO(2,\mathbb C)\simeq\mathbb C^*$, so the endpoint conditions determine the frame twist only up to integer multiples of $2πi$. The difference between the two orientations, $\frac{c_L-c_R}{6}\,iπ$, is fixed entirely by the gravitational anomaly and persists for the Rényi pseudo-entropies.

**Abstract**

We develop a systematic construction of renormalization group (RG) domain walls between two-dimensional rational conformal field theories. Using phantom currents, we consider an auxiliary chiral extension linked to the folded ultraviolet--infrared theory by a topological interface. The construction of RG walls then reduces to identifying suitable automorphism-twisted Cardy states in the auxiliary theory. We also find interesting nontrivial boundary monodromy: a closed cycle in the defect conformal moduli space can carry an RG domain wall into other distinct non-trivial conformal interfaces.
