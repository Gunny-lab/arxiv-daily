**Abstract**

We introduce a simple functional of algebra-valued fields with definite Lorentz properties that is kernel-free yet it encodes the structure of tree-level scattering amplitudes. The fields are evaluated at the points of a piece-wise straight paths in the anti-self-dual plane of complexified Minkowski space, with the directions of consecutive segments correlated so that the path has a velocity memory. We show that momentum-space reduction of the functional generates the cyclic Parke--Taylor factor, off shell in terms of light-front bilinears and on shell in terms of spinor brackets. The same reduction, organized differently, can be naturally promoted to twistor space without any twistor-space input: a closed cyclic chain of Penrose transforms on a twistor line, in which the Parke--Taylor denominators arise as boundary terms of the integrals along the paths. Finally we show that derivatives with respect to one of the path parameter generate numerators that together with the Parke-Taylor denominator build scattering amplitudes. Lorentz invariance fixes the spins of the inserted fields and the homogeneities of the corresponding twistor functions without any use of supersymmetry. The three-point outputs reproduce the kinematic forms of the light-cone cubic vertices of chiral higher-spin theory, and the derivative monomials of order four generate the components of the $\overline{\rm MHV}$ supermultiplet of $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory, off shell and on shell.

**Abstract**

We investigate the polarization of the charged lepton in the semileptonic hyperon decay $Λ\to p\,\ell^-\barν_\ell$ ($\ell=e,μ$) within the Standard Model, using the form factors obtained from lattice QCD. We compute the longitudinal, transverse and normal polarization of the lepton, the forward-backward asymmetry of each lepton spin state, and the lepton flavor universality ratio $R_{μe}$. We show that the longitudinal polarization determines the ratio $g_1/f_1$ of the axial-vector to vector couplings, and that the forward-backward asymmetries of the electron and of negative-helicity muons determine the weak magnetism. The asymmetry of positive-helicity muons is fixed by kinematics up to a small dependence on the second-class form factors, whereas $R_{μe}$ is nearly independent of $g_1/f_1$ and the weak magnetism. For comparison, we also use the QCD sum-rule form factors, whose $g_1/f_1$ and weak magnetism are opposite in sign to experiment and lattice QCD, and find very different values for the observables that depend on them. Moreover, the $q^2$ dependence of $R_{μe}$ fixes the longitudinal polarization of the muon, which can thus be measured from the decay rates, without measuring the muon spin. The normal polarization is generated by the electromagnetic final-state interaction and provides the Standard Model reference for searches for time-reversal violation. The electron forward-backward asymmetry and $R_{μe}$ can be measured at BESIII and the proposed Super Tau-Charm Facility, and $R_{μe}$ also at LHCb. These measurements can test the Standard Model and the lattice-QCD form factors.

**Abstract**

**Abstract**

Matter-wave interferometers (MWIs) are a genuine quantum probe of dark matter (DM), being sensitive to DM-induced phase shifts and loss of contrast. **Abstract**

Forward-backward multiplicity correlations probe the longitudinal structure of particle production, but fluctuations in the number of emitting sources complicate their interpretation. Strongly intensive observables remove the contribution of source-number fluctuations at second order. Here we extend the construction to all cumulant orders. For independent identical sources whose emission law does not depend on the source count, we build a normalized hierarchy of strongly intensive observables with $n$ new algebraically independent quantities at order $n$. Preliminary ALICE measurements at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV are used to illustrate the corresponding model-dependent scenarios.

With an exposure of 1296 kg$\cdot$days reactor-on, a blind likelihood analysis yields $1555 \pm 152$ CE$ν$NS events, with a significance of 10.2 $σ$, in agreement with the Standard Model expectation of $1701 \pm 198$ events within 0.6 standard deviations. The result also determines the weak mixing angle at a momentum transfer of about 10 MeV, $\sin^2θ_W = 0.225 \pm 0.022$, consistent with the Standard Model prediction.

**Abstract**

The latest developments regarding the feasibility of muon-hadron collisions, achieved through the major reuse of LH$e$C infrastructure, are presented - at the LH$μ$C, a 1 or 2 TeV $μ^+$ beam collides with a 7 TeV proton beam, resulting in the $μ^+p$ centre-of-mass energy of 5.3 or 7.5 TeV, respectively, at an average luminosity close to $10^{34}$cm$^{-2}$s$^{-1}$. The paper concludes with an outlook underlying unique and powerful synergies between the LH$μ$C, LH$e$C, FCC and Muon Collider projects.
