### On the origin and geometry of anomaly-related poles in Double Deeply Virtual Compton Scattering

- arXiv: [2611.18386v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18386v1) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18386v1)
- Authors: Shohini Bhattacharya, Alexander Sang Yi

- Relevance score: 49 (high)
- Scoop-risk score: 0/100 (normal)
- Scoop-risk reasons: none
- Matched keywords: title: compton scattering, scattering; abstract: amplitude, amplitudes, compton scattering, polarization, scattering

**Abstract**

Anomaly-related poles have been reported in one-loop box amplitudes for the gluon-initiated channel of double deeply virtual Compton scattering (DDVCS). The residues of these poles carry the gluonic operator structures associated with the chiral and trace anomalies. The origin of the poles within the box amplitude has remained unclear. In this work, we trace both poles to a subleading Landau singularity on the boundary of the box's Feynman-parameter domain, where one parameter vanishes and the remaining three propagators form a triangle subdiagram. We examine the same boundary using dimensional regularization (DR), nonzero momentum transfer $t$, and a finite quark mass $m$. At $t=m=0$, the boundary contribution is power infrared divergent in four dimensions. Analytic continuation in DR conceals this divergence, which gives no $1/ε$ pole about $d=4$. At the critical dimension $d=6$, the same endpoint diverges logarithmically and gives a pole in the continued integral. Nonzero $t$ and finite $m$ regulate the same boundary region, yielding the known anomaly-related $1/t$ pole and a previously unreported $1/m^2$ behavior, respectively. We also show that a transverse gluon-polarization projector can eliminate an anomaly-related contribution that survives with explicit gluon polarization vectors. Finally, we present complete one-loop results for polarized and unpolarized DDVCS with all three infrared prescriptions. After summing the diagrams, the same evolution kernel governs the infrared pole in DR and the logarithms $\ln(Q^2/(-t))$ and $\ln(Q^2/m^2)$ within each polarization channel. This work establishes the common geometric origin of these anomaly-related poles within one-loop box amplitudes.

### Lean-Verified No-Go Theorems for $R$-Symmetries and Modular Symmetries in 4D GUTs

**Abstract**

$R$-symmetries are instrumental in supersymmetric model building. However, a no-go theorem restricts us from using them in a large class of four-dimensional (4D) grand unified theories (GUTs). Specifically, it has been shown that a supersymmetric 4D GUT based on SU(5), with finitely many chiral multiplets, cannot be broken spontaneously to the exact minimal supersymmetric standard model (MSSM) while preserving a discrete $\mathbb{Z}_M^R$, with $M \geq 3$, or continuous U(1) $R$-symmetry. We use Lean 4 to formalize this proof. In the process, we identify and close a gap in the general case of the published argument. Further, we discuss the implications for GUT models with modular symmetries. We point out that modular symmetries are inevitably $R$-symmetries in a supersymmetric framework that also describes gravity. At certain critical points in moduli space, discrete $R$-symmetries are linearly realized. In the context of 4D GUTs, the no-go theorem then implies that exotic states become massless at these points, provided the GUT-breaking vacuum preserves these symmetries. In the vicinity of these critical points, these exotics are parametrically light, which may spoil gauge coupling unification.

**Abstract**

We summarize our recent lattice QCD study of chiral and singlet $U(1)_A$ symmetry breaking in background magnetic fields. The magnetic field explicitly reduces the non-singlet flavor symmetry through the unequal light-quark electric charges. We therefore study two neutral-channel susceptibility differences: $χ_{π^0}-χ_σ$ for chiral symmetry breaking and $χ_{π^0}-χ_{δ^0}$ for singlet $U(1)_A$ symmetry breaking. Magnetic fields enhance both susceptibility differences at low temperatures. Near the crossover, sufficiently strong fields instead suppress both differences. The suppression sets in at larger magnetic fields and is milder for the singlet-axial susceptibility difference than for the neutral chiral susceptibility difference.

**Abstract**

The fuzzy-sphere regularization provides a powerful framework for studying three-dimensional (3D) conformal field theories (CFTs) through radial quantization, but conformal defects have so far been studied with it only by exact diagonalization (ED) of small systems. In this work we realize the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ twist line defect of the 3D Ising CFT on the fuzzy sphere by imposing antiperiodic boundary conditions on the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-odd fermion flavor, and solve it with a multi-target density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) algorithm that optimizes several low-lying states of each symmetry sector in a common matrix-product basis. This gives the defect spectrum at system sizes well beyond the reach of ED (up to $2s+1=25$, or $49$ spinless orbitals). The spectrum obeys the spin-parity selection rule of the twist line and organizes into conformal multiplets along the line. Using the displacement operator as an intrinsic ruler, its protected descendant as a test, and the bulk stress tensor as an independent cross-check, we identify the leading finite-size corrections and obtain controlled continuum extrapolations, which sharpen the dimensions of several known defect primaries and resolve four higher-lying ones. This study establishes the fuzzy sphere combined with multi-target DMRG as a framework for precision studies of conformal defects.

**Abstract**

We compute the entropic order parameter introduced in (1905.10487) using the replica trick for two disjoint intervals. For a global symmetry, this quantity measures the mutual information carried by charge-anticharge intertwiners. We formulate the problem in terms of averages over twisted partition functions on replica Riemann surfaces, and obtain exact solutions for free fermions and compact bosons, for $U(1)$ symmetry and its discrete $\mathbb Z_N$ subgroups. We show that further averaging over both families of non-contractible cycles gives access to an additional quantity, which is the loss of Holevo information associated with complementary-charge measurements that occurs upon removing the intertwiners. For the boson, this quantity vanishes, whereas for Dirac fermions it equals the difference between the mutual information of the fermion-parity-gauged and ungauged theories, independently of the choice of even $\mathbb Z_N$ subgroup. Finally, we extend the analysis to non-Abelian symmetries using saddle expansions, deriving universal behavior in the nearly touching intervals limit and for a large number of fermions $N_F\gg1$.

**Abstract**
