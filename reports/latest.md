**Abstract**

We introduce a simple functional of algebra-valued fields with definite Lorentz properties that is kernel-free yet it encodes the structure of tree-level scattering amplitudes. The fields are evaluated at the points of a piece-wise straight paths in the anti-self-dual plane of complexified Minkowski space, with the directions of consecutive segments correlated so that the path has a velocity memory. We show that momentum-space reduction of the functional generates the cyclic Parke--Taylor factor, off shell in terms of light-front bilinears and on shell in terms of spinor brackets. The same reduction, organized differently, can be naturally promoted to twistor space without any twistor-space input: a closed cyclic chain of Penrose transforms on a twistor line, in which the Parke--Taylor denominators arise as boundary terms of the integrals along the paths. Finally we show that derivatives with respect to one of the path parameter generate numerators that together with the Parke-Taylor denominator build scattering amplitudes. Lorentz invariance fixes the spins of the inserted fields and the homogeneities of the corresponding twistor functions without any use of supersymmetry. The three-point outputs reproduce the kinematic forms of the light-cone cubic vertices of chiral higher-spin theory, and the derivative monomials of order four generate the components of the $\overline{\rm MHV}$ supermultiplet of $\mathcal N=4$ super Yang--Mills theory, off shell and on shell.

**Abstract**

We obtain a formula relating the covariant Wigner function to the mean spin vector for a weakly interacting field. **Abstract**

Inelastic scattering from $N$ coherent spins, or any two-state system, scales as $N^2$, invalidating the Born approximation at large $N$ even at weak coupling. **Abstract**

Mutual Coulomb dissociation in ultra-peripheral heavy-ion collisions is dominated by Giant Dipole Resonance (GDR) excitation, whose subsequent decays produce forward neutrons. Standard descriptions treat the two GDRs excited in opposite nuclei as statistically independent, leading primarily to a quadrupolar azimuthal correlation determined by the common photon-polarization axis. In this work, we investigate the possibility that the two GDRs are instead prepared with a correlated quantum phase during the overlap of the electromagnetic pulses. We develop a phase-sensitive GDR decay model and implement it in a modified version of the \texttt{$n_{0}^{0}n$} Monte Carlo generator, allowing a quantitative comparison among three competing mechanisms: the weak dipolar modulation from electromagnetic recoil, the quadrupolar correlation from independent polarized GDR decays, and the phase-sensitive dipolar modulation associated with the proposed phase-lock scenario. For neutron pairs with small relative emission-time differences, the correlated-phase scenario produces a pronounced first-harmonic modulation in the two-neutron relative-azimuth distribution. Averaging over all relative emission times substantially reduces this modulation. Within the adopted energy-dependent angular model, the time-selected dipolar coefficient increases in magnitude with pair-mean neutron kinetic energy. These results identify neutron relative-azimuth correlations and their energy dependence as experimentally accessible observables for probing quantum phase correlations between the two GDR excitations. Such phase-sensitive correlations could also provide a basis for future tests of quantum entanglement between spatially separated GDR excitations at RHIC and the LHC.

**Abstract**

Forward-backward multiplicity correlations probe the longitudinal structure of particle production, but fluctuations in the number of emitting sources complicate their interpretation. Strongly intensive observables remove the contribution of source-number fluctuations at second order. Here we extend the construction to all cumulant orders. Preliminary ALICE measurements at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV are used to illustrate the corresponding model-dependent scenarios.

**Abstract**

The thickness of the neutron skin ($Δr_{np}$) in $^{48}$Ca remains uncertain. CREX and ab initio calculations point to a thin skin, while many energy density functionals prefer larger values. Relativistic heavy-ion collisions can probe the skin through its geometric imprint on the quark-gluon plasma, but a thicker skin also changes the overall nuclear size, so the two effects are hard to separate. We resolve this by systematically comparing $^{48}$Ca+$^{48}$Ca and $^{48}$Ti+$^{48}$Ti collisions at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=5.02$ TeV using the AMPT model with string melting. For $^{48}$Ca, we take a zero-skin baseline and skins at the CREX lower and upper bounds. As size references, we add $^{48}$Ti and a skin-free $^{48}$Ca with the same density parameters as $^{48}$Ti, which separates size from skin effects. The sensitivity to the $^{48}$Ti radius is tested using two choices for its Woods-Saxon radius, one of which reproduces the experimental rms radius. We find that the neutron skin is a purely geometric perturbation; the medium's transport response coefficients are unchanged, first-moment observables exhibit only modest sensitivity to $Δr_{np}$, while second-moment observables respond much more strongly. The final-state covariance between flow and mean transverse momentum fluctuations, with its normalized correlation, is the most promising measurable probe. The radius-matched $^{48}$Ca follows $^{48}$Ti closely, and the position of $^{48}$Ti relative to the skin configurations depends on its radius. These collisions, analyzed with second-moment and shape-size correlators, offer a promising independent constraint on the $^{48}$Ca neutron skin at the LHC.

**Abstract**

In theories with compact extra dimensions, the four-dimensional effective description is obtained by Kaluza-Klein reduction. The state available to a four-dimensional observer is the reduced density matrix obtained by tracing the higher-dimensional vacuum over the tower of massive modes, and it need not be pure. In this paper we study the momentum-space entanglement of this state for a five-dimensional scalar on $M_4\times S^1$ with a cubic or quartic self-interaction. We compute, at leading order in the coupling, the mutual information $I_n$ between the zero modes below a resolution scale $μ$ and a single Kaluza-Klein level $n$. When the zero modes couple linearly and without derivatives to pairs of tower modes, $I_n$ falls as $1/m_n$, where $m_n$ is the mass of level $n$. In the cubic theory, this tail is independent of the ultraviolet cutoff, with coefficient $(μR)^2/1024π^3$ for compactification radius $R$, and the mutual information with the whole tower grows logarithmically with the number of levels. In the quartic theory, the tail is masked by states containing additional hard quanta, which make $I_n$ depend on the cutoff. Compared with the corrections the tower makes to low-energy amplitudes, which are dominated by the lightest levels and captured by local operators, the mutual information with the tower is not dominated by any finite set of levels and arises from the time-nonlocal part of the effective action. The local four-dimensional effective theory therefore describes the low-energy observables of the reduced system but not its state. Unbroken supersymmetry cancels the vacuum energy of the compactification but not the mutual information, which at leading order is a sum of squared amplitudes.
