**Abstract**

Inelastic scattering from $N$ coherent spins, or any two-state system, scales as $N^2$, invalidating the Born approximation at large $N$ even at weak coupling. We develop a nonperturbative theory of this superradiant scattering and its backreaction on the spins. Since one particle can exchange many quanta, the target's energy-transfer and energy-variance rates keep their favorable $N^2$ scaling even after the cross section saturates, unless a conservation law limits each encounter to exchange of one quantum. We apply the theory to millicharged-particle searches, as well as neutron and charged-particle beams that can test quantum protocols for detecting cosmic relics.

**Abstract**

Mutual Coulomb dissociation in ultra-peripheral heavy-ion collisions is dominated by Giant Dipole Resonance (GDR) excitation, whose subsequent decays produce forward neutrons. Standard descriptions treat the two GDRs excited in opposite nuclei as statistically independent, leading primarily to a quadrupolar azimuthal correlation determined by the common photon-polarization axis. In this work, we investigate the possibility that the two GDRs are instead prepared with a correlated quantum phase during the overlap of the electromagnetic pulses. We develop a phase-sensitive GDR decay model and implement it in a modified version of the \texttt{$n_{0}^{0}n$} Monte Carlo generator, allowing a quantitative comparison among three competing mechanisms: the weak dipolar modulation from electromagnetic recoil, the quadrupolar correlation from independent polarized GDR decays, and the phase-sensitive dipolar modulation associated with the proposed phase-lock scenario. Averaging over all relative emission times substantially reduces this modulation. Within the adopted energy-dependent angular model, the time-selected dipolar coefficient increases in magnitude with pair-mean neutron kinetic energy. These results identify neutron relative-azimuth correlations and their energy dependence as experimentally accessible observables for probing quantum phase correlations between the two GDR excitations. Such phase-sensitive correlations could also provide a basis for future tests of quantum entanglement between spatially separated GDR excitations at RHIC and the LHC.

**Abstract**

**Abstract**

The thickness of the neutron skin ($Δr_{np}$) in $^{48}$Ca remains uncertain. CREX and ab initio calculations point to a thin skin, while many energy density functionals prefer larger values. Relativistic heavy-ion collisions can probe the skin through its geometric imprint on the quark-gluon plasma, but a thicker skin also changes the overall nuclear size, so the two effects are hard to separate. We resolve this by systematically comparing $^{48}$Ca+$^{48}$Ca and $^{48}$Ti+$^{48}$Ti collisions at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=5.02$ TeV using the AMPT model with string melting. For $^{48}$Ca, we take a zero-skin baseline and skins at the CREX lower and upper bounds. As size references, we add $^{48}$Ti and a skin-free $^{48}$Ca with the same density parameters as $^{48}$Ti, which separates size from skin effects. The sensitivity to the $^{48}$Ti radius is tested using two choices for its Woods-Saxon radius, one of which reproduces the experimental rms radius. We find that the neutron skin is a purely geometric perturbation; the medium's transport response coefficients are unchanged, first-moment observables exhibit only modest sensitivity to $Δr_{np}$, while second-moment observables respond much more strongly. The final-state covariance between flow and mean transverse momentum fluctuations, with its normalized correlation, is the most promising measurable probe. The radius-matched $^{48}$Ca follows $^{48}$Ti closely, and the position of $^{48}$Ti relative to the skin configurations depends on its radius. These collisions, analyzed with second-moment and shape-size correlators, offer a promising independent constraint on the $^{48}$Ca neutron skin at the LHC.
