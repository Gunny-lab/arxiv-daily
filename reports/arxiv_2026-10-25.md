**Abstract**

We present an updated global analysis of the transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) helicity distributions of the nucleon from measurements of the longitudinal double-spin asymmetry $A_{LL}$ in semi-inclusive deep-inelastic scattering (SIDIS). The analysis incorporates target- and detected-hadron-mass corrections, next-to-next-to-leading-order matching in the small-$b_T$ operator product expansion, and an improved treatment of correlated systematic uncertainties. The present data constrain the up- and down-quark helicity TMDs, whereas the sea-quark and gluon distributions remain weakly constrained. After integration over transverse momenta up to the hard scale, the up- and down-quark distributions agree with collinear helicity distributions within uncertainties. Using the extracted TMDs and the Wandzura-Wilczek-type approximation, we predict the $\cosφ_h$ modulation of $A_{LL}$ and find agreement with the HERMES and CLAS measurements. We also provide predictions for SIDIS $A_{LL}$ at CLAS12 and for longitudinal single-spin asymmetries in $W^\pm$ production with leptonic decays at STAR kinematics. An impact study with pseudodata for the proposed Electron-Ion Collider in China (EicC) indicates substantial potential to improve constraints on helicity TMDs at intermediate $x$, including the presently weakly constrained sea-quark sector.

**Abstract**

The effective field theory (EFT) of contact forces which describes few-nucleon systems at distance scales larger than the pion Compton wavelength ${\rm EFT}({π\hskip-0.54em /})$ can be defined in terms of momentum-dependent potentials that contain a fixed number of simple poles and generate the effective-range expansion to a given fixed order. These potentials provide a practical generalization of the standard ${\rm EFT}({π\hskip-0.54em /})$ power-counting scheme which, for instance, naturally accounts for the large effective ranges in the s-wave channels. Nucleon-nucleon phase shifts computed in this formulation of ${\rm EFT}({π\hskip-0.54em /})$ are compared to modern phase-shift analyses, and effective-range parameters are tabulated. The ${\rm EFT}({π\hskip-0.54em /})$ potentials, which provide a rational approximation of the S-matrix, are easily extended into the regime of pion physics through the inclusion of additional S-matrix poles and zeros, and provide an accurate description of data up to pion-production threshold. The potentials in this scheme are in an important sense weak over all relevant distance scales, and are therefore of utility for systematic calculations of many-nucleon observables.

**Abstract**

Primordial tensor non-Gaussianity can probe massive particles present during inflation. We investigate this possibility in an effective field theory where the massless graviton mixes with the helicity-two mode of an additional massive spin-two field $σ_{ij}$ with cubic self-interactions. For constant sound speed, we compute the leading tensor power-spectrum correction and the tree-level bispectrum, finding novel analytical expressions for these quantities. In the $m_σ>3H/2$ regime, we find a nonanalytic squeezed contribution that exhibits logarithmic oscillations with characteristic dependence $(k_L/k_S)^{3/2\pm iμ}$, where $μ=\sqrt{m_σ^2/H^2-9/4}$. The oscillation frequency is set by the mass $m_σ$, while the polarisation contractions determine the relative amplitudes of the different graviton helicity configurations. The same mixing generates corrections to the power spectrum and the bispectrum. We derive the associated quadrupolar modulation of short-scale tensor power spectrum and relate its amplitude to the model parameters. Finally, we explore how a slowly varying sound speed modifies the squeezed signal.~These results connect the two-point, three-point, and quadrupolar tensor signatures of massive spin-two fields in the cosmological gravitational wave collider.

**Abstract**

We show how Fricke modular symmetry can generate realistic fermion hierarchies and quark CP violation from a single modulus in a supersymmetric effective theory. Matter fields are labelled by the two one-dimensional characters of $Γ_0^+(N)$ that are trivial on $Γ_0(N)$, and interactions use complete spaces of cusp-regular holomorphic modular forms. At level three, the Fricke extension enlarges an interior stabilizer from order three to order six, allowing its parity to enforce three additional powers of the displacement $t$. An explicit assignment gives quark mass powers $(7,3,0)$ and $(5,4,2)$ and CKM powers $(1,2,3)$. The leading textures conserve CP, but an exact relation among the modular generators produces an appreciable CKM phase at finite displacement. A fit at $|t|\simeq0.306$ reproduces the quark and lepton reference values with order one coefficients in a fixed normalization. We also relate the Fricke double-coset algebra to coset non-invertible symmetry; on the coefficient spaces used here its action reduces to ordinary Fricke parity.

**Abstract**

This thesis investigates the perturbative dynamics and phenomenology of compact objects in two approaches to quantum gravity: quadratic gravity and asymptotic safety. Linear perturbations provide a tool to probe the physical properties of spacetimes and their potential observational signatures, including quasinormal modes, black hole shadows, and Hawking radiation. The first part focuses on Einstein-Weyl gravity, where an additional massive spin-2 degree of freedom modifies the dynamics of compact objects. We derive and analyze tensor perturbation for static, spherically symmetric solutions, identifying Gregory-Laflamme-type instabilities in the monopole sector and studying the time domain evolution of higher multipoles. We also investigate universal massive-field tails and the breakdown of the standard eikonal correspondence between quasinormal modes and null geodesics. Particular attention is devoted to veiled singularities, horizonless configurations that satisfy a form of weak cosmic censorship and can mimic black hole shadows. The second part examines regular black holes in asymptotically safe gravity. Using the proper-time flow approach, we construct and investigate a dynamical regular black hole solution arising from gravitational collapse. We analyze its perturbative stability and quasinormal mode spectrum, finding characteristic deviations from Schwarzschild black holes. We explore the phenomenological implications of quantum corrections for shadows and Hawking radiation. While shadow remains compatible with current constraints, the modified evaporation process features lower temperatures and suppressed emission rates, with implications for primordial black holes and their possible contribution to dark matter. Overall, this work demonstrates how the perturbative dynamics of compact objects can connect quantum gravity models to potentially observable astrophysical phenomena.

**Abstract**

In this thesis we compute the impulse of a classical black hole scattering encounter in the Post-Minkowskian (PM) expansion - a weak-field expansion in Newton's constant $G$ - up to the fifth order. We describe the black holes as point particles within an effective field theory framework and couple them through gravity. The classical equations of motion are solved diagrammatically using the worldline quantum field theory (WQFT) formalism, in which the solution with causal boundary conditions is encoded in tree-level Feynman diagrams built from graviton and worldline fields. The Feynman integrals arising at each order are reduced by advanced IBP reduction techniques and evaluated with the method of differential equations. We find that, in the solution of these integrals, periods over Calabi--Yau manifolds - familiar from string theory - appear and contribute to the classical impulse at the fifth Post-Minkowskian order. The results of this work are the first analytic results for the scattering observables at fifth Post-Minkowskian order, together with the appearance of Calabi--Yau threefold periods in a classical physics observable.

**Abstract**

Carrollian geometry has recently emerged as the proper language to understand the geometry of null hypersurfaces from an intrinsic perspective. In this paper, we demonstrate that the Noether charge associated with the intrinsic conformal symmetry of Carrollian manifolds yields exactly the dynamical entropy formula recently proposed by Hollands, Wald and Zhang. This result follows directly from the requirement that the gravitational phase space induced on a finite distance null hypersurface be anomaly-free, in a covariant phase space sense. We then show that a linearized perturbation of this dynamical entropy charge, sourced by an external matter perturbation falling across the hypersurface, satisfies a physical process first law. By imposing the quantum null energy condition (QNEC), we find a generalized second law for a linearized perturbation of the dynamical generalized entropy. Finally, we use our results to speculate on relations to the quantum focusing conjecture.

**Abstract**

We compute the static contribution to the conservative two-body potential in massless scalar-tensor theories through fifth post-Newtonian (5PN) order, with the strong-field scalar responses of the compact objects encoded in scalar-dependent masses.
