**Abstract**

**Abstract**

We investigate the sensitivity to the dimension-six Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) operators which define the interactions of heavy-quarks with electroweak gauge bosons and with leptons via the process $μ^- μ^+ \rightarrow Wtb$, followed by leptonic W-boson decays, at a 10 TeV muon collider with an integrated luminosity of 10 ab$^{-1}$. **Abstract**

We present the first complete dimension-eight implementation of the geometric Standard Model Effective Field Theory (geoSMEFT) as UFO models for event generators such as MadGraph. The models contain all 674 baryon-number-conserving operators, with minimal flavour violation for the fermionic operators, and are also provided in the Murphy basis. We include dimension-six contributions and electroweak input-parameter corrections consistently through order $1/Λ^4$, and provide dimension-eight squared contributions as a diagnostic of EFT truncation. We validate the implementation against independent calculations and Ward identities. To make these models practical for collider studies, we develop a standalone tool that identifies all operators contributing to a given process at tree level, including those entering through input-parameter shifts. It reports their expected energy scaling and generates a restriction card to reduce the computational cost of event generation. The same geometric construction allows us to calculate two- and three-point functions beyond dimension eight, to arbitrary order in $1/Λ^2$. The models and the tool are publicly available.

### Small-$x$ helicity evolution with gluon saturation

**Abstract**

We include gluon saturation in the revised small-$x$ helicity evolution at large $N_c$. Keeping the unpolarized dipole $S$-matrices, set to one in the double-logarithmic approximation, we derive the evolution equations for the polarized dipole amplitudes $G$, $G_2$ and their neighbor amplitudes. The equations reduce exactly to the known ones for $S=1$ and contain new terms proportional to $1-S$, including one in the equation for $G_2$ arising because the real and virtual contributions that cancel for $S=1$ are weighted differently by the $S$-matrix of the small daughter dipole; this term is ultraviolet finite and acts only for a saturated daughter dipole. Inside the saturation region the growth rate of $G$ drops from $3.66$ to $2\sqrt{1-λ}$, in units of $\sqrt{α_sN_c/2π}$, with $λ$ the growth exponent of the saturation scale, while $G_2$ oscillates without growing. In the asymptotic regime, the gluon helicity $ΔG$ reaches a maximum and decreases, while the quark helicity $ΔΣ$ and $g_1$ keep growing with an exponent set by the dipoles at the edge of the saturation region, obtained in closed form; saturation raises the critical $α_s$ below which the integral of $ΔΣ$ converges from $0.156$ to $0.21$ for $λ=0.25$. For $10^{-2}\ge x\ge10^{-6}$ we solve the equations numerically with a target described by the running-coupling Balitsky-Kovchegov equation, with a saturation scale close to the one extracted from HERA data. At the reach of the Electron-Ion Collider, $x\sim10^{-4}$, and $Q^2=10$ GeV$^2$, saturation reduces $ΔG$ by a factor of about three and $ΔΣ$ by about two; both still grow, more slowly. Comparing three choices of the starting point, $x_0=0.1$ and $0.01$, we find that this dependence is lost toward $x=10^{-6}$.

**Abstract**

Superconducting qubits provide microwave-scale transitions and thus offer quantum targets for direct particle scattering with extremely small energy transfer. We develop a general framework for inelastic scattering in which an incident particle exchanges an off-shell photon with a superconducting quantum circuit, inducing a transition from a lower-energy internal state of the circuit to a higher-energy one. The target dependence is encoded in a finite-momentum electromagnetic transition current and, for charge-dominated response, in a qubit transition form factor. A key feature of particle scattering is that a microwave-scale excitation does not necessarily correspond to a long-wavelength interaction, since nonrelativistic particles can transfer spatial momenta much larger than the deposited energy. The full finite-momentum device response is therefore essential. We specialize the formalism to transmon qubits, construct a realistic electrostatic response for a planar Xmon-style transmon, and apply it to photon-mediated scattering of eV-scale bosonic dark matter, including final-state Bose stimulation. We find that the resulting enhancement substantially improves the projected sensitivity at the lowest masses. Owing to the exceptionally low transition threshold of superconducting qubits, this approach opens sensitivity to dark matter in the eV-to-keV mass range, which remains largely unexplored by existing direct-detection experiments. We further show that $N$ independently operated qubits give the expected rate proportional to $N$, while suitably prepared Dicke states can in principle achieve a collective enhancement approaching $N^2$ when spatial coherence is maintained across the array. These results establish superconducting qubits as quantum targets for direct particle scattering and provide a framework for optimizing low-threshold dark-matter searches.

**Abstract**

We investigate the production mechanism of $K^-N\to K^*Ξ$ and the role of high-mass excited hyperons within an effective Lagrangian approach, using a combined fit to cross section and polarization data for $K^-p\to K^{*+}Ξ^-$. **Abstract**

Massive neutron stars (NSs) require a sufficiently stiff core equation of state (EOS), while the relatively small tidal deformability inferred from GW170817 favors softer nuclear matter at intermediate densities. Given the importance of the nuclear symmetry energy in neutron-rich matter, an intriguing question arises: Can the symmetry energy become supersoft at high densities while remaining compatible with these seemingly competing constraints? These results connect the microscopic nucleon momentum distribution with the density dependence of the symmetry energy and macroscopic NS properties.

**Abstract**

In the eternal AdS black hole, the entanglement of the thermofield double state is geometrically realised by the Einstein--Rosen bridge, in line with the ER=EPR conjecture. Hartman--Maldacena surfaces threading this bridge pass behind the event horizon, so the time-dependent entanglement entropy $S(t)$ of a pair of half-lines, one on each boundary, probes the black hole interior. We turn this relation around and ask how much of the bulk spacetime can be reconstructed from $S(t)$, using a neural ordinary differential equation that learns the metric directly from the entanglement data. Our results thus offer a data-driven perspective on how spacetime, including the black hole interior, emerges from quantum entanglement, and delineate which features of the geometry entanglement alone can determine.

**Abstract**

We consider what the entanglement between two regions of a critical system can reveal about its symmetries, including non-invertible ones. For two intervals in a two-dimensional conformal field theory, the second Rényi entropy is a partition function on a torus, and a topological defect line can be wrapped around one of the intervals. For an on-site symmetry this computes the overlap of the reduced density matrix with its image under the symmetry acting on one interval, and in general it defines an observable that depends only on the cross-ratio. We also show that the associated twist-field crossing equations are a form of the modular bootstrap, and discuss whether the full fusion category can be recovered from entanglement on surfaces of higher genus.
