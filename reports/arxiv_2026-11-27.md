**Abstract**

In the framework of $2\to 2$ relativistic scattering described by the perturbative $S$-matrix, we perform a comprehensive analysis of the properties of quantum channels obtained by tracing out the momentum from the scattered state. We work in two distinct scenarios with qubit degrees of freedom: flavored scalar contact interactions and QED, focusing in particular on initial-state leptons with transverse polarization. We identify the conditions that make the quantum channel unitary, calculate the entanglement of the qubits through the concurrence, and use its maximally allowed value to extract perturbativity upper bounds on the coupling constants. We find that the limits are often more constraining than the corresponding partial-wave expansion bounds, and that they allow us to bypass some of the obstacles associated in the latter with the singular behavior of forward scattering.

**Abstract**

Searches for New Physics at the energy frontier have motivated theoretical development of model-independent methods to constrain interactions beyond the Standard Model (BSM) like the Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT), as well as associated phenomenological analyses. While these approaches are powerful, they are also afflicted by the large space of higher-dimensional operators and the challenge of possible degeneracies among these operators. To help ameliorate these difficulties, processes beyond those at hadron colliders, including deep-inelastic scattering (DIS), have been identified and studied for their ability to contribute added constraining power. We explore projections for future (proposed) facilities like the EIC, LHeC, and FCC-eh, finding that improvements to nuclear parton distribution functions (nPDFs) can further sharpen the reach of nuclear DIS.

**Abstract**

The description of heavy quarks inside the QGP medium, especially at low momenta, remains challenging from first principles due to the strong coupling and large gluon occupation numbers. An alternative description in terms of perturbative degrees of freedom treats the interactions of heavy quarks with the collective excitations of the medium in the form of phonons. This description is derived as an effective field theory (EFT) and therefore allows for a naturally occurring power counting of the allowed interactions. This EFT has already been established in our previous work, where we derived the phonon-heavy quark interactions and calculated their thermal scattering cross section. In this work, we use these results, together with the heavy quark expansion, to calculate the heavy quark drag coefficient. Subsequently, we compare our results with the drag coefficient obtained from AdS/CFT calculations to determine the previously unknown Wilson coefficients for the quark-phonon coupling. Additionally, we determine the same Wilson coefficients by matching the EFT result for the drag force at low momenta with the QCD result at high momenta. Finally, we will compare our results with experimentally determined heavy quark diffusion coefficients.

**Abstract**

We derive the dimensional reduction of 10D super Yang-Mills theory on a general closed 6D spin manifold using spectral decompositions for the higher-dimensional gauge field and gaugino, keeping the infinite towers of Kaluza-Klein (KK) modes in the lower-dimensional theory. We then calculate tree-level $2 \rightarrow 2$ scattering amplitudes with external massive spinors and vectors in this lower-dimensional theory. By studying the high-energy behavior of these amplitudes and imposing perturbative unitarity, we derive several sum rules that involve the eigenvalues of Laplace and Dirac operators on the internal 6-manifold, together with products of cubic coupling constants of the KK modes. These give concrete realizations of the known unitarity sum rules for spontaneously broken gauge theories. We illustrate explicitly how the simplest sum rule is satisfied for flat tori and round spheres.

**Abstract**

Parton distribution functions (PDFs) and light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) encode the non-perturbative structure of hadrons in terms of their partonic constituents. They are defined through matrix elements containing parton operators that are connected by a Wilson line along a light-front direction, which makes them difficult to access in Euclidean lattice calculations. We demonstrate how parton functions can be directly obtained in Minkowski space using the Hamiltonian framework. We compute fermion and antifermion PDFs, LCDAs, and decay constants of the vector meson in the Schwinger model for several fermion masses. States in Hilbert space are efficiently represented by tensor networks, with an expressivity that is systematically controlled by a bond dimension. The light-front Wilson line is implemented as a stepwise evolution on the lattice that alternates spatial shifts of an external charge with Trotterized time-evolution steps acting on a matrix product state. All systematic uncertainties are quantified by varying and extrapolating in the lattice spacing, volume, bond dimension, gauge-field truncation, and Trotter step width. The slow growth of the entanglement entropy makes moderate bond dimensions sufficient, which supports the feasibility of using tensor networks for computing light-front matrix elements in more general settings. We confirm that the computed distributions fulfill the expected normalizations, momentum sum rules, and symmetry properties.

- Authors: Sebastian Garcia-Saenz, Yizhou Lu, Sébastien Renaux-Petel

- Scoop-risk reasons: none
- Matched keywords: title: collider, effective field theory; abstract: collider, effective field theory, eft, observables, symmetry

**Abstract**

We study a massive field strongly mixed with the curvature perturbation during inflation, in the regime where the latter is described at low energies by a single-field effective field theory (EFT) with a quadratic dispersion relation. What this EFT captures, and what lies beyond it, are encoded in a connection problem, which relates the solutions that define the vacuum at early times to those that describe observables at late times. We solve it with a general method that does not rely on an exact solution. An expansion in the inverse mixing, keeping both fields, yields the EFT as its leading order, with corrections under systematic control. It selects the vacuum of the EFT, fixes a phase that the EFT cannot determine on its own, and describes the heavy mode at all times. Particle production lies beyond all orders of this asymptotic expansion. We show that it arises from a Stokes phenomenon at the complex branch points of the dispersion relation and that, in a rotating basis of the two fields, it reduces to the familiar production of particles with half the heavy mass. A reflection symmetry of the equations of motion, combined with the canonical commutation relations, then determines all exponentially small effects exactly, and with them the solution of the connection problem, which combines particle production with a conversion between light and heavy quanta. Together with the expansion, these exact results give a compact analytical formula for the cosmological collider signal, whose exponential suppression, half of the usual Boltzmann one, directly reflects the halved mass of the produced particles. The expansion only requires a separation of scales and the adiabaticity of the heavy mode, while the reflection symmetry holds for many systems in de Sitter space. We therefore expect this approach to bring physical understanding and simple formulas well beyond the present case.

**Abstract**

We investigate the information paradox for one-sided asymptotically flat rotating Kerr black holes formed through gravitational collapse using the island prescription. Near the horizon, the dynamics of a scalar field can be described by an effective two-dimensional theory, which provides the framework for our analysis. We study how rotation affects the location of the island boundary $\partial I$, the Page time, and the scrambling time, treating non-extremal and extremal Kerr black holes separately. For non-extremal black holes, the emergence of an island prevents the unbounded growth of Hawking radiation entropy at late times and restores the unitary Page curve. Our results demonstrate the important role of black hole geometry in determining the behaviour of entanglement entropy and suggest that the island prescription provides a semiclassical resolution of the information paradox for one-sided rotating Kerr black holes.

**Abstract**

Continuous global symmetries can obstruct logarithmic-depth symmetric unitary $k$-designs and pseudorandom unitaries (PRUs) through long-range correlations. We ask whether gauge symmetries impose analogous limitations. For $\mathrm{U}(1)$ lattice gauge theories, we identify a randomization scale $c$, the minimum patch size needed for matter to screen every allowed boundary-flux mismatch, and a diffusive scale governing boundary-flux correlations. We conjecture that this condition allows gauge-symmetric systems to scramble as rapidly as unconstrained ones and support this by constructing logarithmic-depth approximate strong designs and strong PRUs.

Mueller dipole branching at small $x$ is a stochastic process in which splitting rates depend on transverse geometry. We construct a coherent quantum circuit for a regulated finite-state version of this process. Poisson-clock uniformization controls the number of updates, while orthogonal event-history registers retain the splitting paths, including those leading to the same final configuration. These results demonstrate an explicit coherent encoding of finite-state dipole branching and clarify its relation to continuous-cascade fluctuations and DIS phenomenology.

**Abstract**

We review the chiral anomaly from a historical perspective, from the $π^0\toγγ$ puzzle and the work of Adler, Bell and Jackiw, to the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) term. We then describe how the WZW term was motivated from quantum chromodynamics in 1983-85. Integrating out the non-Abelian gauge fields, that have a gapped spectrum, gives a Nambu-Jona-Lasinio type theory of quarks, and integrating out the quarks, which are gapped by chiral symmetry breaking, gives the WZW model. The fermion determinant splits into a regulator-dependent modulus, which gives the dynamics of mesons, and a universal phase, which is fixed by the anomaly and is the WZW term. Restricted to the collective coordinates of the baryon soliton, the WZW term imposes a U(1) constraint that makes the baryon wave functions monopole harmonics and fixes their spin, statistics and flavour multiplets. We then explain that this phase is a Berry phase: the Schwinger terms of current algebra are the curvature of a U(1) connection on the space of chiral field configurations, and the WZW term is its holonomy, which we call a Berry-Wilson loop. In 1+1 dimensions we write the connection explicitly in terms of Euler angles, exhibiting a Dirac monopole whose quantised charge is the level of the current algebra.

**Abstract**

We show that in any $d$-dimensional CFT, the vacuum sandwiched Rényi mutual information (SRMI) of integer order $n$ between two disjoint balls maps to a CFT partition function on a manifold of topology $\#^{n-1}(S^1 \times S^{d-1})$, with moduli fixed by the ball configuration and single-ball modular flow. At $n=2$, it is given by the thermal partition function on $S^{d-1}$, upper-bounding the von Neumann mutual information. For $n>2$, SRMI diverges at finite separation due to a hot-spot limit of the partition function. Explicit CFT evaluations agree well with density matrix computations.

**Abstract**

It has been emphasized recently that some extremal black holes are "critical" solutions, lying at the threshold between gravitational collapse and dispersion. We study the effect of quantum gravity on critical solutions for the collapse of a thin shell of charged dust. We consider the Hamiltonian that generates the shell equation of motion with respect to the exterior Killing time and coincides with the ADM energy on-shell. Near criticality, this Hamiltonian is shown to be canonically equivalent to that of a Liouville theory with negative potential. After quantization, this model predicts that a collapsing shell has a non-trivial probability of undergoing a quantum mechanical bounce when its energy above extremality is comparable to the "breakdown scale". The bounce probability approaches unity at extremality. Hence this model predicts that the critical surface in the classical theory is thickened quantum mechanically into a "critical layer". The probability density function for the black hole energy is suppressed below the breakdown scale in the same way as found recently by Turiaci and Wu.

**Abstract**

We propose a microscopic mechanism for screening the cosmological constant through quantum chaos of the horizon degrees of freedom. We show that in a recently proposed quantum-horizon matrix model arXiv:2406.01466, the Schwarzschild--de Sitter junction completion arXiv:2608.21178 supports a gravitationally dressed soft mode that approaches the massless minimally coupled scalar of de Sitter gravity. Interactions among the microscopic horizon degrees of freedom provide a mechanism for force relaxation. Assuming a finite-memory force spectrum with nonzero regular low-frequency weight, the gravitationally dressed soft mode undergoes Brownian diffusion after momentum relaxation. The resulting bosonic coarse-grained entropy supplements the background entropy arising from fermionic parton microstates. We propose that their sum defines the total thermodynamic entropy, which can be identified with the Gibbons--Hawking entropy of the backreacted causal horizon. Entropy generation during the diffusion regime then leads to infrared screening of the effective cosmological constant, with a universal entropy--variance coefficient agreeing with that of Kitazawa and collaborators. Radiative lifting of the soft mode provides a possible mechanism for terminating the screening regime.
