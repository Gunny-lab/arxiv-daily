**Abstract**

Spin-spin correlations of a quark-antiquark pair produced in proton-proton or proton-Ion collisions are investigated in $k_T$-factorization within the shockwave formalism. We construct the full spin-density matrix of both inclusive and diffractive channels in the limit in which the two quarks are back-to-back. In the inclusive channel, due to linearly polarized gluons, we show that a $\cos (2 φ)$-modulation of the concurrence, stabilizer Rényi entropy, and quantum discord emerges and depends on the interplay between six small-$x$ gluon TMDs. The modulation is predicted to be maximally strong and independent of the transverse momentum imbalance, $q$, from a dilute (BFKL) perspective, while it is considerably suppressed by saturation effects. In diffraction, we observe that the pair is always entangled and exhibits the same properties found in diffractive DIS. We explain how these properties follow directly from the exclusive and eikonal nature of the Pomeron exchange. We trace this behavior to a spin-density matrix of rank at most two with maximally mixed marginals. For this class of states, entanglement and Bell nonlocality coincide even when the state is mixed, while the explicit form of the light-front helicity amplitudes ensures that both persist in the whole phase space. We suggest accessing the spin correlations in unpolarized hadron collisions through the Collins and dihadron fragmentation functions. We point out that, in inclusive production, for massless quarks, suitably chosen double-Collins asymmetries provide promising entanglement witnesses.

**Abstract**

A central question in holography is how different boundary observables encode and reconstruct the geometry of the gravitational dual. In this work, we uncover an exact geometric correspondence between two seemingly distinct aspects of strongly coupled dynamics: the dissipative drag force experienced by a moving heavy particle(quark) and the entanglement membrane governing the propagation of quantum information. We show that both constructions retain the same underlying structure of the geometry, leading to an invertible transformation between the finite-velocity drag force and the geometric membrane tension. This correspondence reveals that nonlinear momentum loss encodes detailed information about entanglement dynamics: the leading nonlinear correction determines the butterfly velocity, while higher-order terms successively probe the local geometry of the membrane near the horizon. We further connect this hierarchy to scalar pole-skipping, establishing an all-order relation between nonlinear dissipation and near-horizon spectral data. The shared geometric structure also has implications for quantum information recovery, with the quintic drag coefficient determining the leading dependence of information velocity on initial entanglement. Conversely, the complete information-velocity curve provides a reconstruction of the reduced bulk geometry along the physical interior membrane branch, complementing the exterior reconstruction from drag. We show that stochastic momentum broadening supplies the additional radial information needed to reconstruct the complete exterior metric up to an overall scale. Our results thus reveal how dissipation, entanglement dynamics, and spectral properties provide complementary access to holographic geometry, establishing a unified framework for understanding the geometric information encoded in distinct boundary observables.

### Renormalized symmetry probes for emergent symmetries and non-chiral lattice fermions

**Abstract**

The degeneracy of hadron correlators in symmetry-related channels signals symmetry restoration in high-temperature QCD. RG-invariant probes of this degeneracy need care when the regularization breaks chiral symmetry, or when the symmetry is not exact in the massless action, as for the emergent chiral-spin symmetry $SU(2)_{CS}$. We renormalize the thermal and screening meson correlators of every flavor sector by the thermal correlator of the same channel in the $\bar ud$ sector at a common reference point. The symmetry ratio $κ_{AB}$ then depends on the renormalization only through the ratio $R_{AB}$. For exact symmetries, $R_{AB}=1$ and $κ_{AB}$ is RG invariant and scheme independent in mass-independent schemes. For emergent symmetries, $R_{AB}\neq1$ carries the relative logarithmic cutoff dependence of the two channels, which cancels in $κ_{AB}$, and the residual dependence on the reference scale is physical. The framework applies to Wilson, twisted-mass and staggered fermions, where $R_{AB}$ absorbs the channel-dependent renormalization, and the tensor pair $(T_k,X_k)$ keeps $R_{T_kX_k}=1$ on isotropic Wilson-type lattices. Lattice artifacts are a small correction to the chiral-spin probes $κ_{CS}$ and $κ_{fade}$, but as large as the signal of the exact-symmetry probes. For free quarks, we derive $κ_{fade}=2/3$ for every mass and separation, and $κ_{CS}\le1/3$. We apply the renormalized probes to $N_f=2+1+1$ QCD with optimal domain-wall quarks at the physical point, with $a=0.064$ fm and $T=192$-$1539$ MeV. The hierarchical restoration of the chiral symmetries from the light to the charm sectors is confirmed, and is invariant under renormalization. The $\bar uc$ and $\bar sc$ sectors have the widest chiral-spin windows, also at a tighter threshold. Thus spin-1 mesons with one charm quark are the most attractive channels for the chiral-spin regime.

**Abstract**

We study negative-parity hidden-flavor pentaquarks with three to five heavy quarks, $QQ\bar{Q}qq$, $QQQ\bar{Q}q$, and $QQQQ\bar{Q}$ with $Q=c,b$ and $q=u,d$, in QCD sum rules, using the complete set of S-wave baryon--meson currents with $J^P=1/2^-$, $3/2^-$, and $5/2^-$ and the operator product expansion up to dimension 10, 9, and 6. Currents whose baryon and meson share no quark flavor are exactly factorizable at this order and cannot distinguish bound states from two-hadron states.

**Abstract**

The spin correlation of $Λ\barΛ$ hyperon pairs produced in heavy-ion collisions is investigated through scalar and pseudo-scalar mesonic channels that mediate the creation of $s\bar{s}$ pairs. Trajectories passing near the crossover region exhibit a non-monotonic structure, providing a sensitive probe for the location of the chiral phase transition.

**Abstract**

We present a systematic treatment of entanglement in two-dimensional topological quantum field theories (TQFTs). Our approach clarifies the sensitivity of entanglement measures to the cutting maps that implement Hilbert space factorization. Such maps embed the TQFT Hilbert space into a description with potentially more degrees of freedom, and for entanglement to be well defined they must be isometric, i.e., they must preserve the norms of all states. We show that the naïve cutting map based solely upon path integration subject to boundary conditions is not isometric for generic TQFTs. For any 2d TQFT, we give a prescription to construct isometric cutting maps by dressing path integration with suitable edge operators. This allows us to compute entanglement for any state, any bipartition, and any boundary condition. As a consequence, we find a non-integer "ground-state degeneracy" à la Affleck-Ludwig, despite all boundary conditions being gapped. We further show how to reconstruct the partition function of the TQFT at arbitrary genus from the multi-interval Rényi entropies of a single state. For theories with (possibly non-invertible) symmetries, we show that the entanglement entropy of the symmetric ground state can distinguish gapped phases with the same symmetry, without any further knowledge of their vacua, while the entanglement asymmetry of the vacua entropically detects symmetry breaking. We illustrate our formalism with a range of applications, from finite gauge theories to Haagerup gapped phases and infrared phases of adjoint QCD$_2$.

**Abstract**

While the entanglement entropy of a spatial subregion has been extensively studied, a properly defined entanglement entropy of quantum states in a time direction interval remains underexplored.
